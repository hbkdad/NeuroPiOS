# Economic Attack Analysis

Status: Phase 1 design-time analysis + Phase 2 (partial) simulation. Two of the attacks below (self-dealing reward farming, Sybil reputation farming) are actually exercised as automated tests in `simulations/poig_sim/tests/` — see results in the milestone report. The remainder are design-time analysis only, not yet simulated, and are marked as such.

| Attack | Description | Mitigation | Simulated in Phase 2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-dealing jobs | Actor creates jobs paid to itself with no real external demand | `UsefulWorkScore` requires escrow-backed signed job from a distinct requester identity with independent standing; `DemandFactor` capped | **Yes** — `tests/test_self_dealing.py` |
| Fake demand / wash activity | Coordinated actors create real-looking but mutually-arranged job flow to inflate apparent network demand | `DemandFactor` cap + requester-reputation gating; graph analysis (planned, not built) as a longer-term defense | No — requires multi-identity relationship modeling beyond this milestone's scope |
| Reward farming (generic) | Any strategy that maximizes `Reward` without maximizing genuine `UsefulWorkScore × QualityFactor` | Addressed structurally by the multiplicative reward formula itself — a zero in any bounded factor zeroes the reward | Partially — covered by the self-dealing test |
| Sybil farming | One actor creates many identities to bootstrap reputation cheaply or to out-vote validators | Bonding cost, max verification rate for new identities, non-transferable/non-purchasable reputation, decay | **Yes** — `tests/test_sybil_cluster.py` |
| Validator bribery | A requester or worker pays validators off-protocol to approve a false result | Commit/reveal prevents copy-voting; quorum weighting limits any single validator's influence; reputation consequence for detected bad votes | Partially — commit/reveal's copy-vote prevention is simulated (`tests/test_validator_collusion.py::test_commit_reveal_prevents_copying_another_validators_vote`); off-protocol side-payment modeling itself is out of scope for an economic simulator |
| Collusion (validator cartel) | A validator subset systematically agrees regardless of ground truth | Commit/reveal + weighted quorum resists a **minority** colluding cluster; a **majority** colluding cluster can still flip a quorum outcome by construction (no quorum-weighting scheme can prevent this) — mitigated only by layering L6 on top of L2/L4 evidence and reputation/bonding consequences, not by quorum weighting alone | **Yes, both directions** — `tests/test_validator_collusion.py`: minority collusion blocked (`test_minority_collusion_does_not_flip_the_outcome`), majority collusion succeeds and is honestly documented as a residual risk (`test_majority_collusion_does_flip_the_outcome_documented_residual_risk`) |
| Stake concentration | A small number of actors accumulate enough stake to dominate quorums/governance | Governance timelock + multisig (contract-layer, Phase 10); quorum design should weight participation, not raw stake, where feasible — open design question, not fully resolved | **Yes** — `tests/test_stake_concentration.py` isolates weight concentration from headcount directly: a 2-of-10 dishonest minority with concentrated stake (weight 40 vs. the honest majority's 8) dominates the quorum outcome, while the identical 2-of-10 headcount split with equal weights does not, and the flip point is shown to track the whales' aggregate weight *share* crossing 50%, not their headcount. Confirms this is a genuinely unresolved design gap (as the mitigation column already says) rather than something quorum weighting alone protects against |
| Reputation farming | Gaming the reputation formula directly (e.g., many trivial low-risk jobs to pad completion-reliability) | Reputation is multi-dimensional (not a single gameable scalar) and per-job-type; low-value job floods are rate-limited and contribute proportionally less | Partially covered by the Sybil test (which is a specific case of this) |
| Model plagiarism | Republishing another creator's weights under a new manifest to capture royalties | Content-addressing means the underlying weights CID is identical/derivable even under a new manifest wrapper — detectable, not automatically prevented; provenance disputes need a resolution process (not yet specified) | No — flagged as an open spec gap |
| Benchmark leakage | Hidden benchmark answers extracted via repeated submission probing | Rotating benchmark suites, multiple metrics, rate-limited submission attempts per identity | **Yes** — `tests/test_benchmark_leakage.py`: a real aggregate-score-only bit-flip probing attack (`poig_sim/benchmark.py`) fully recovers a 30-item hidden answer key when unmitigated (100% accuracy, confirming the attack is real, not a strawman), but stays at chance-level accuracy (well under 80%, typically 25-70%, across 10 tested seeds) when rate limiting + rotation are active, since the attacker never gets the `n+1` submissions needed to complete the probe before the answer key rotates out from under them |
| Front-running | Observing a pending job/settlement transaction and acting on it before it confirms | Mitigated at the settlement-layer choice (L2 sequencer characteristics, ADR-0001) more than at the protocol layer; not yet analyzed in depth | No |
| Price manipulation | Coordinated bidding to distort the routing engine's `priceEfficiency` signal | Routing score combines multiple weighted signals (not price alone), reducing single-signal manipulation leverage; weights themselves need real-data tuning | **Yes** — `tests/test_price_manipulation.py` confirms a pure price-maximizing manipulator cannot outscore an established honest worker, and that single-signal leverage is mathematically capped at that signal's own configured weight share (checked for all six signals, not just price) |
| Griefing | Deliberately wasting counterparties' resources without a clear profit motive (e.g., always disputing to force bisection cost) | L4's bisection cost falls disproportionately on the losing party in a dispute, disincentivizing frivolous challenges; exact cost allocation is a simulation-tunable parameter | No |
| Treasury drain | Governance or admin-key compromise draining protocol treasury | Multisig + timelock + pause capability at the contract layer (Phase 10, not yet built) | No — contracts don't exist yet |

## What "simulated" means here, precisely

Every row marked "Yes" (or "Partially") runs as real `pytest` tests against real (if simplified) Python logic in `simulations/poig_sim` — not hand-waved. As of milestone 7 that's: self-dealing reward farming, Sybil reputation farming, validator collusion (both minority-blocked and majority-succeeds cases), commit/reveal anti-copy-voting, price manipulation, benchmark leakage (both the unmitigated attack succeeding and the mitigated case failing), and stake concentration (weight-share dominance isolated from headcount) — 22/22 tests passing, reported honestly including the attacks (majority validator collusion, stake-concentrated minority) that actually succeed against the current design. Every other row above remains design-time analysis pending future simulation work — explicitly not claimed as tested.
